\begin{abstract}
We describe our submission to Track~2 (Dr.DocBench) of the DocInsights~2026 shared
task, which converts single document-page images into structurally faithful
Markdown, scored on Text edit distance, Table TEDS, Formula CDM, and Reading Order.
Our system is a hosted vision--language model (VLM) pipeline: Qwen3.8-max as the
primary parser, with a Gemini~3.7-Flash ensemble routed to table pages. Rather than
switching models or engineering ever-larger prompts, we found that the single most
effective lever was \emph{image fidelity}: upscaling the low-resolution scans to the
VLM encoder's pixel budget and encoding them at maximum JPEG quality together
improved Overall by $+1.67$, twice the gain of every other change combined. On the
EvalAI Public Validation set (509 pages) our system reaches an Overall of
\best{71.33}. We report an extensive set of \emph{negative} results---reading-order
reordering, learned reading-order models, document-class conditioning, and
column-cropping all failed---and the lightweight validation discipline that let us
identify each failure on a held-out split before spending a leaderboard submission.
Code is publicly available.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/jinyichao/DocBench}}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Expert-level document parsing---faithfully converting a scanned page into
structured Markdown---sits at the intersection of OCR, layout analysis, and
structured extraction. Dr.DocBench \citep{yang2026dr}, the Track~2 benchmark of
the DocInsights~2026 shared task, is deliberately \emph{difficulty-aware}: its pages
were selected around cases where strong document parsers struggle, spanning dense
tables, irregular multi-column layouts, formulas, chemistry notation, and musical
scores. Each page is scored on four components---Text edit distance, Table TEDS,
Formula CDM, and Reading Order---converted to a $0$--$100$ scale and averaged per
page; the Overall is the mean of per-page scores over all scorable pages.

Our guiding question was pragmatic: given a strong hosted VLM, where does the
remaining Overall actually come from? We answer it with a disciplined ablation
campaign in which every candidate change was validated on a held-out development
split before consuming a leaderboard submission. The headline finding is that the
gains do \emph{not} come from model selection or prompt sophistication, both of
which we found largely saturated. They come from \textbf{feeding the model
higher-fidelity images}. The Dr.DocBench scans are low-resolution (median long edge
$968$\,px); at native resolution, dense text is under-tokenized by the VLM's
patch-based encoder. Upscaling each page to the encoder's pixel budget and encoding
at maximum JPEG quality raised Overall from $69.65$ to $71.33$.

Our contributions are: (i) a simple, reproducible pipeline that reaches Overall
$71.33$ on Dr.DocBench Public Validation; (ii) the empirical finding that image
fidelity---not model or prompt---is the dominant lever for low-resolution document
parsing, together with a measurement of the VLM's internal resolution ceiling;
and (iii) a documented set of negative results and the validation methodology that
produced them, which we believe is the most transferable outcome for future
participants.

\subsection{Engines}
Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} shows the end-to-end flow.
The primary parser is \textbf{Qwen3.8-max} \citep{qwen38} accessed through the
DashScope OpenAI-compatible endpoint, with internal ``thinking'' disabled (parsing
does not benefit from a hidden scratchpad, and disabling it is faster and cheaper).
We add a second engine, \textbf{Gemini~3.7-Flash} \citep{gemini}, used in two narrow
roles: (a) an ensemble
component routed to table pages, where we found it consistently stronger on TEDS,
and (b) a recovery path for pages that DashScope's account-level content filter
hard-blocks (returning \texttt{data\_inspection\_failed}), which we otherwise emit
as empty Markdown.

\subsection{Prompt Design}
\label{sec:prompt}
Both engines share a single prompt, tuned to the scorer's output conventions rather
than to human readability; the full text is reproduced verbatim in
Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}. Key decisions, each validated on dev:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Tables} are emitted as raw HTML with \texttt{<td>} for every cell
    (never \texttt{<th>}, which the TEDS normaliser collapses anyway), the exact row
    $\times$ column grid, and no in-cell \texttt{<br>}; hyphen-split words are
    rejoined. This table block alone added ${\approx}5$ TEDS on dev over a
    naive HTML instruction.
  \item \textbf{Formulas}: isolated equations in display delimiters
    (\texttt{\$\$\ldots\$\$}); inline math in \texttt{\textbackslash(\ldots
    \textbackslash)}.
  \item \textbf{Chemistry}: \texttt{\textbackslash ce\{\ldots\}} for expressions and
    fenced \texttt{smiles} blocks for drawn 2D structures. The scorer treats
    chemistry as \emph{text}, so we do not wrap it in math delimiters.
  \item \textbf{Reading order}: column-major (finish the left column before the
    right). We verified on dev that ground-truth reading order is column-major on
    $35$ of $38$ clean two-column pages, matching the model's default.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Image Encoding: The Core Lever}
\label{sec:image}
Our encoder resizes each page so the long edge is $2000$\,px (upscaling low-resolution
scans, not only downscaling large ones) and encodes to JPEG at quality~$100$ with
chroma subsampling disabled. Two mechanisms explain the gains:

\paragraph{Clearing the visual-token floor.} VLM encoders tile an image into
fixed-size patches; text that spans too few patches cannot be resolved. Upscaling a
$968$\,px scan to $2000$\,px raises the number of visual tokens devoted to the same
(interpolated) glyphs, letting the encoder separate characters it previously smeared
together. This adds no new pixel information but removes a tokenisation bottleneck.

\paragraph{The resolution ceiling.} Qwen applies an internal \texttt{max\_pixels}
cap. We measured the billed input tokens as a function of the long edge
(Table~\ref{tab:tokens}): tokens grow up to $2000$\,px and then plateau---$2500$\,px
and $3000$\,px yield the \emph{identical} token count, i.e.\ the model silently
downscales them to the same image. Thus $2000$\,px is exactly the sweet spot, and
input resolution beyond it is discarded. Requesting a larger budget server-side
(\texttt{vl\_high\_resolution\_images}) does raise the token count but did
\emph{not} improve scores, because the native scans carry no detail beyond the cap.
Within the fixed pixel budget, however, JPEG quality still matters: raising quality
from $90$ to $100$ reduces compression artefacts on small glyphs and improved both
Text and Reading Order on the test set.

\subsection{Ensemble Routing and Assembly}
A layout detector \citep[DocLayout-YOLO;][]{zhao2024doclayout} flags table pages; on those pages we replace the
Qwen output with Gemini's when Gemini emits a valid, non-truncated \texttt{<table>}
of comparable length (a guard against under-generation). Content-filter--blocked
pages are filled from the Gemini recovery run. The remaining pages keep the Qwen
output. The assembled tree is packaged into the required \texttt{predictions.jsonl}
after validation against the manifest.

\subsection{Robustness}
Larger upscaled payloads make hosted calls prone to hanging connections; we set a
per-request timeout so a stalled call aborts and retries, and wrap batch inference in
a self-healing supervisor that restarts on lack of progress until every page is
complete. Inference is resume-safe (a page is skipped if its Markdown already
exists).

\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
We evaluate on the EvalAI Public Validation phase ($509$ required pages, of which
$487$ are scorable). For local iteration we use a held-out development split with
released ground truth, reusing the official DrDocBench metrics (Text edit distance,
TEDS \citep{zhong2020image}, reading-order edit distance) and an offline Formula CDM
\citep{wang2024cdm} renderer
(\LaTeX\,$\rightarrow$\,PDF\,$\rightarrow$\,Hungarian character matching). All
design decisions were made against dev; the leaderboard was used only to confirm
banked improvements.

\subsection{Submission Trajectory}
\label{sec:trajectory}
Figure~\ref{fig:subs} plots every submission we scored on the leaderboard, both the
five we kept and the two we discarded. Panel~(a) is the Overall trajectory; panel~(b)
breaks it into the components that carry signal on this split (Text, reported as

it is $0.43$ for the first two submissions and $0$ thereafter, because the split
contains essentially no isolated display formulas (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}).

Three things are visible in the trajectory. First, the two ensemble/prompt steps move
Table TEDS ($+0.84$) but barely touch Text or Reading Order, which is why they buy so
little Overall---both metrics apply to every page, while TEDS applies only to table
pages. Second, the image-fidelity steps move exactly those high-coverage components:
upscaling adds $+1.94$ Text and JPEG~q100 adds a further $+0.74$ Text and $+0.78$
Reading Order. Third, the discarded submissions are informative. The column-crop
ensemble ($67.30$) regressed by $2.35$ because detector false positives split
single-column pages; and reverting the $13$ low-resolution table pages to quality~$90$
($71.11$) recovered $+0.50$ TEDS but lost $0.31$ Text and $0.16$ Reading Order---one
transcription per page means the q100 table dip cannot be separated from the
text and order gains on the same pages. The \texttt{smiles} step is the smallest
of the five ($+0.01$): it changes only the five pages of the split's single chemistry
handbook, where a drawn ring structure now yields a fenced \texttt{smiles} block
containing \texttt{O=CN1CCCCC1} instead of being skipped or redrawn as ASCII art.

\section{Analysis and Lessons Learned}
\label{sec:analysis}

\paragraph{Image fidelity dominates.} For low-resolution scans, the bottleneck is
how much of the page the encoder can resolve, not the model's parsing ability. The
two fidelity steps outweighed every prompt and ensemble change combined, and the
resolution ceiling (Table~\ref{tab:tokens}) tells us precisely when the lever is
exhausted.

\paragraph{Exploiting scorer structure.} The Text metric penalises \emph{missing}
ground-truth content but not \emph{extra} predicted blocks (unmatched predictions
are discarded, not penalised). This asymmetry justifies an ``emit when unsure''
policy---e.g.\ transcribing text embedded in figures, or emitting a \texttt{smiles}
block for every drawn structure---since surplus content is free while omission is
costly.

\paragraph{Negative results.} Several intuitive levers failed, each caught on dev:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Reading order} resisted every approach we tried: a stronger
    column-discipline prompt (net-negative), geometric XY-cut ($78.4$ even on
    ground-truth boxes, below the model's $81.85$), an off-the-shelf learned
    reading-order model \citep[LayoutReader;][]{wang2021layoutreader} (far below both), and routing to Gemini
    (worse on non-table multi-column prose). Ground-truth order depends on page
    \emph{content}, which the VLM already sees; a box-only model cannot beat it.
  \item \textbf{Formula CDM} is near-zero on this test set---but a layout-detector
    sweep confirmed the Public Validation split contains essentially \emph{no}
    isolated display formulas, so the metric is a near-empty sample, not a failure.
    Forcing display-math would only mis-categorise inline chemistry.
  \item \textbf{Document-class conditioning} and same-class few-shot exemplars
    appeared to help but were traced to same-document leakage; the honest
    cross-document effect was neutral.
  \item \textbf{Column cropping} (detect columns, transcribe each separately)
    regressed, because detector false positives split single-column pages.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Validation discipline.} The recurring lesson is that a cheap held-out
evaluation---matched to the deployment condition (detector-selected, not
oracle-selected, pages; cross-document, not same-document, exemplars)---is worth far
more than leaderboard probing. Every negative above was identified on dev before
spending a submission, and two would have been actively harmful had we deployed them
on the basis of a flawed validation.

\section{Conclusion}
Our Dr.DocBench system reaches Overall $71.33$ with a deliberately simple recipe: a
strong hosted VLM, a scorer-tuned prompt, a narrow table-page ensemble, and---above
all---high-fidelity image encoding calibrated to the model's resolution ceiling. The
broader message for expert-level document parsing on low-resolution scans is that
the image, not the model, is often the binding constraint. We release our code and a
record of the negative results to help future participants spend their effort where
it pays off.

\section*{Limitations}
Our Overall is reported on the Public Validation split, whose composition (prose-heavy,
no display formulas) differs from the full benchmark, so component scores are more
informative than the aggregate. The pipeline depends on two proprietary hosted APIs,
whose behaviour (content filtering, internal \texttt{max\_pixels} cap) may change and
is not fully controllable. Reading order remains our weakest scorable component, and
we did not pursue model fine-tuning.

\section{Prompts}
\label{app:prompts}
This appendix gives the complete prompts, verbatim as sent to both engines. The
system prompt and the user prompt are identical for Qwen3.8-max and
Gemini~3.7-Flash; only the image encoding and the API client differ. The prompt is
a single static string---there is no per-page conditioning, no few-shot exemplar,
and no retry-time prompt variation (a retry re-sends the same two messages). Each
request is one user turn containing the page image followed by the text of
Appendix~\ref{app:user}.

\subsection{System prompt}
\label{app:system}
\begin{lstlisting}[style=prompt]
You are an expert document-parsing engine. You convert a single scanned document page image into faithful, structurally accurate Markdown. You transcribe exactly what is on the page: no translation, no summarizing, no commentary, no invented content. You never omit body content and never add content that is not visible.
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{User prompt (final system)}
\label{app:user}
This is the prompt used for every reported result. The only block we ever rewrote is
TABLES; the note after the listing explains it and gives the earlier, naive version it
replaced. All other blocks (READING ORDER, TEXT, FORMULAS, CHEMISTRY, CODE, MUSIC,
FIGURES, OUTPUT) were fixed from the first submission on.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=prompt]
Convert this document page into a single Markdown document that preserves both its text content and its structure. Follow these rules EXACTLY.

READING ORDER
- Emit blocks in natural human reading order: for multi-column layouts, finish the left column top-to-bottom before the right column. Interleave captions/footnotes at the position they belong to in the flow.

TEXT
- Transcribe the original language and script verbatim. Do not translate or normalize spelling.
- Preserve paragraphs; separate distinct blocks with a blank line.
- Use Markdown headings (#, ##, ...) for titles/section headings.
- For illegible characters, transcribe your best reading of what is visible; do not guess whole words that are not there.

FORMULAS
- Isolated / display equations: wrap in display delimiters on their own line, e.g.
  $$ E = mc^2 $$
- Inline math: use \( ... \).
- Use standard LaTeX. Do not wrap formulas in code fences.

TABLES
- Output every table as raw HTML: a single <table> containing <tr> rows, and <td> for EVERY cell (use <td> even for header cells — do not use <th>).
- Reproduce the exact grid you see: the same number of rows and the same number of columns. One <td> per visual cell. Represent an empty cell as <td></td>.
- Use rowspan / colspan ONLY where one cell visibly spans multiple rows or columns in the image; otherwise omit them. Never split one real cell into several <td>, and never merge separate cells into one.
- Put each cell's entire text on a single line. Do NOT place <br>, <p>, <div>, <span>, or newlines inside a cell. If a word or number is wrapped across two printed lines (often with a hyphen at the break), rejoin it into one continuous token (e.g. "product-order-\nletter" -> "product-order-letter").
- Transcribe cell text exactly, including inline math as \( ... \). Do not add, drop, reorder, or re-align columns.
- Do NOT use Markdown pipe tables. Do NOT wrap the table in a code fence.
- If a table continues from/to an adjacent page, still emit the portion visible on THIS page as one <table>.

CHEMISTRY
- Chemical reactions/equations: use mhchem, e.g. \ce{2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O}.
- Drawn 2D molecular structure diagrams (skeletal/ring structures, e.g. under a "Structural Formula" heading): output the molecule as a SMILES string inside a ```smiles fenced block. Do NOT redraw it as ASCII art, and do NOT skip it. If the compound is also named on the page, use the name to produce the correct SMILES.
- Molecular structures already given as SMILES text: put the SMILES string verbatim in a ```smiles fenced block.

CODE / PSEUDOCODE
- Put code or algorithm listings in a fenced code block with a language tag, e.g. ```python or ```pseudocode.

MUSIC
- Transcribe musical scores as MusicXML inside a ```musicxml fenced block. Do not include the <?xml?> declaration or the <score-partwise> wrapper element.

FIGURES / IMAGES
- Do not output image markdown or describe purely pictorial figures.
- DO transcribe any text embedded in a figure (labels, axis ticks, legends, callouts) as plain text at the figure's position.

OUTPUT
- Output ONLY the Markdown for the page. No preamble, no explanation, no ``` fence around the whole answer.
- If the page is blank, output nothing.
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent The TABLES block is the only one we rewrote against the metric rather than
for readability, so it is worth a note. TEDS compares the predicted table as a tree
(grid, spans, cell strings), and a structural error is expensive in a way a
typographic one is not: one cell too few shifts every later cell in that row into the
wrong column, whereas a misspelled word costs only that cell's string similarity. The
block above therefore fixes what the naive instruction left open---\texttt{<td>} for
every cell so headers are never a decision, the exact grid with \texttt{<td></td>} for
empty cells, spans only where a cell visibly spans, and no \texttt{<br>}/\texttt{<p>}
inside a cell (which would both add nodes and split a token the ground truth holds as
one string). Swapping the naive block for it, everything else fixed, is the
${\approx}5$ TEDS of Section~\ref{sec:prompt} (dev: $50.1 \rightarrow 55.1$); we never
spent a submission on the naive block, so that figure is a dev measurement. That block
kept the raw-HTML, no-pipe-table and continued-table rules unchanged, but on structure
said only ``use \texttt{<tr>}/\texttt{<td>}/\texttt{<th>} with \texttt{rowspan} and
\texttt{colspan} to reflect merged cells'' and ``transcribe the cell contents
faithfully''---silent on empty cells, on spurious spans, and on in-cell line breaks.

\noindent The READING ORDER block never changed: the column-major instruction is what
we ship, and Section~\ref{sec:analysis} covers the reordering alternatives we tried
and rejected.

\paragraph{What drives the input cost.} Input tokens are a function of the encoded
resolution, not of file size: the same page at JPEG quality~$90$ and quality~$100$
bills identically, which is why the q100 change in Section~\ref{sec:image} is free.
Upscaling is not free---the native-resolution dev pass bills $1447$ input tokens per
page against $3290$ for the upscaled configuration, roughly $2.3\times$, which moves a
page from $0.64$ to $1.15$ US cents---but it saturates at
the encoder's cap (Table~\ref{tab:tokens}), so the extra cost is bounded and buys the
$+1.03$ Overall reported in Section~\ref{sec:image}. Gemini bills roughly half as many
input tokens per page as Qwen for the same image, a consequence of a different visual
tokeniser rather than of anything we control.

\paragraph{Latency and failure handling.} Per-request latency is dominated by output
generation, so the slowest pages are the dense tables that emit the most tokens. We
cap each request at $120$\,s and retry up to five times with exponential backoff;
larger upscaled payloads made hung connections common enough that without the cap a
single stalled request could hold a worker indefinitely. A supervisor script restarts
a pass that stops making progress, and because inference is resume-safe (a page whose
Markdown already exists is skipped), a restart costs only the in-flight pages---which
is why the page counts in Table~\ref{tab:cost} are not multiples of the split sizes.

\section{Request Parameters}
\label{app:params}
Both engines are called through an OpenAI-compatible chat-completions interface with
one image per request and provider-default sampling (we set no temperature or
top-$p$). The token budget is $8192$ for Qwen3.8-max and $16000$ for
Gemini~3.7-Flash. Images are encoded as described in Section~\ref{sec:image}---long
edge resized to $2000$\,px, JPEG quality~$100$ with chroma subsampling
disabled---and passed as a base64 \texttt{data:} URL in an \texttt{image\_url}
content part preceding the text of Appendix~\ref{app:user}. Transient failures are
retried up to five times with exponential backoff and identical messages; no prompt
text changes between attempts.
